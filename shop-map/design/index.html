01Principles

Research inputs: Mapbox's store-locator guidance (branded markers, list + map in sync, mobile first), Mapped's logo-pin markers and logo filter chips, and UTMB's own "UTMB Friendly" partner list, which is a plain text list with no map. This design keeps the list-and-map pairing and makes the logo the marker.

The logo is the pin

Every logo is fitted into the pin's circle by measuring its content, so round badges fill the disc and wide wordmarks fit by their corners. Nothing is cropped. A shop without a logo gets a navy monogram.

Gold means privilege

Gold appears only on the perk badge, the privilege ticket, selection and the main roads you navigate by. Navy carries structure. Category is shown by a ring color and an icon badge together, so it never depends on color alone.

Three languages, one identity

The shop's name stays exactly as written on its logo. Under it, the name in the reader's language, or in the other script when they match. Offers, categories, map labels and UI switch together.

02Brand mark

A navy pin-shaped seal: the Doi Suthep ridge, the golden chedi on its summit and the dotted trail that runners climb to reach it. The pin outline echoes the shop pins, so the app's mark reads as the first pin on the map.

04Type

Trirong (Thai + Latin) gives shop names and headings a Lanna-manuscript serif. IBM Plex Sans Thai sets every control and line of UI. Chinese switches to Noto Serif SC and Noto Sans SC so the weights stay matched.

05Pins

46 px logo disc, a white ring, a category-colored ring, an icon badge below the disc and a stem. Selected pins grow to 60 px with a gold ring and a label. Search dims non-matching pins instead of hiding them, so you keep your bearings.

  1. Logo disc: the logo pre-fitted to a 288 px square on its own background color, so the circle never shows a mismatched edge.
  2. Ring: 2 px white separates any logo from the map. The 2 px category ring sits outside it.
  3. Badge: category icon on its hue, hung below the disc so it overlaps the rings and never the logo. It carries the category for color-blind users.
  4. Tail: a short stem under the badge. Its tip is the exact coordinate, so the anchor is the bottom of the pin, not the circle center.
